\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Background}

Automated facial analysis systems, covering perceived gender classification, age estimation, and race or ethnicity prediction, are now embedded across commercial and public-sector applications ranging from photo tagging and demographic analytics to surveillance and access control. As these systems have proliferated, a substantial body of empirical research has documented that many of them exhibit statistically significant accuracy disparities across demographic subgroups, particularly by skin tone, gender presentation, and age. The foundational audit by Buolamwini and Gebru demonstrated that leading commercial gender-classification services performed markedly worse on darker-skinned female subjects than on lighter-skinned male subjects, with error rates as high as 34.7\% for the worst-performing intersectional subgroup against as low as 0.8\% for the best-performing one \cite{buolamwini2018gendershades}. That finding catalysed a wider research agenda into fairness auditing for computer vision, subsequently surveyed comprehensively by Dehdashtian et al., who catalogue the sources of bias that arise across the machine learning pipeline, from data collection and annotation through model training and deployment, and the corresponding families of fairness metrics and mitigation techniques proposed in response \cite{dehdashtian2024survey}.

Despite this growing awareness, the computer-vision literature contains comparatively few reusable workflows for systematic, reproducible audits of facial-analysis models. Dehdashtian et al.'s survey describes a substantial body of fairness metrics and mitigation techniques, while noting less work on standardising the auditing workflow itself \cite{dehdashtian2024survey}.

The regulatory context is relevant but does not make an audit report a compliance determination. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 amended the application timetable for high-risk-system requirements under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: the relevant Chapter III requirements apply from 2~December~2027 for Annex~III systems and 2~August~2028 for Annex~I systems. Article~10 has no single, independent application date; the applicable date depends on the system category and the amended timetable \cite{euaiact2024,euomnibus2026}. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) is a complementary voluntary framework, organised around Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage \cite{nist2023aiframework}.

\section{Problem Statement}

This project addresses the engineering challenge of combining fairness metrics, benchmark datasets, and statistical analysis in a reusable workflow for facial-analysis systems. Existing studies, including Gender Shades, evaluate particular systems and datasets; a repeatable software workflow could help researchers conduct comparable evaluations. Such outputs can contribute traceable research evidence, but do not by themselves satisfy regulatory obligations or demonstrate compliance.

\section{Objectives}

\subsection{General Objective}
To design and propose a diagnostic software platform, BiasAperture, for evaluating demographic accuracy disparities in third-party facial-analysis models and reporting traceable research evidence in a standardised format.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To design a modular software architecture that accepts a trained facial analysis model, or a file of its precomputed predictions, together with a demographically labelled benchmark dataset, and computes subgroup and intersectional fairness metrics from it.
    \item To specify the use of AIF360 and Fairlearn as separate implementations of fairness metrics, alongside statistical testing, and to document the limitations of those measures and their uncertainty.
    \item To generate a standardised, exportable fairness report, structurally informed by the Model Cards \cite{mitchell2019modelcards} and Datasheets for Datasets \cite{gebru2018datasheets} documentation conventions, that communicates subgroup performance disparities in a form accessible to both technical and non-technical stakeholders.
    \item To specify a case study using a publicly available, demographically annotated benchmark; FairFace is the retained dataset \cite{karkkainen2021fairface}, and UTKFace was excluded from completed scope.
    \item To propose a crosswalk from fairness metrics to relevant provisions of Article~10 and Annex~IV of the \gls{eu} \gls{ai} Act and to relevant outcomes in the \gls{nist} \gls{ai} \gls{rmf}, as research traceability rather than proof of compliance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Limitations}

\subsection{Scope}
The proposal scopes BiasAperture as a diagnostic and evaluative platform: (i) subgroup and intersectional evaluation of predictions from a facial-analysis classifier; (ii) four disparity metrics, \gls{dpd}, \gls{eod}, \gls{eop}, and \gls{dir}, with statistical tests and confidence intervals; (iii) demographic-dummy logistic-surrogate attribution, which describes associations between demographic indicators and model predictions rather than explaining image content or identifying causal or proxy-confounding mechanisms; and (iv) a proposed Model-Cards- and Datasheets-informed \gls{html} report with a regulatory crosswalk presented as research traceability. FairFace is the retained benchmark \cite{karkkainen2021fairface}; UTKFace was excluded from the completed scope.

\subsection{Limitations}
BiasAperture does not mitigate disparities, retrain models, or generate synthetic demographic data. Results depend on benchmark-label quality; FairFace is the retained dataset and UTKFace was excluded from completed scope. The subgroup interval implementation currently uses a percentile bootstrap with default \(B=200\); it does not meet NFR-002's planned minimum of 1{,}000 resamples, notwithstanding the separate global-row BCa implementation. The demographic logistic surrogate uses demographic indicators and model predictions, so it cannot explain pixels, establish causation, identify proxy confounders, or decompose observed disparities. The planned report is intended to provide traceable research evidence, not legal advice, a regulator-ready dossier, or proof of compliance. Runtime and other performance values in this report are targets unless identified as measured.
