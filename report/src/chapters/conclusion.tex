\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This report presents the proposal, requirements, and architecture for BiasAperture, a diagnostic platform for evaluating demographic accuracy disparities in third-party facial-analysis models. It reviews the fairness literature and regulatory sources, then describes a proposed workflow, a FairFace-only benchmark scope, and a research-oriented regulatory crosswalk. UTKFace was excluded from the completed scope. These design and literature-review contributions do not establish that the proposed platform has met every objective or regulatory obligation. The subgroup confidence-interval implementation uses percentile bootstrap with default \(B=200\), and adapters do not pass a larger budget; this falls short of proposed NFR-002's minimum of 1{,}000 resamples. The separate global BCa routine does not remedy that subgroup gap. Fairlearn and AIF360 are metric-computation backends, not independent assessors as described by NIST AI RMF Measure 1.3.

The proposed system is diagnostic: it reports measured disparities and uncertainty but does not remediate them, retrain a model, or generate synthetic demographic data. Its report is intended to provide traceable research evidence and must not be described as regulator-ready, a compliance dossier, or proof of compliance.

\section{Future Work}

The proposal's descoping order in \cref{tab:cutlist} records planned options for the project. UTKFace was excluded from completed scope and is not part of the current case study; any future evaluation would require a separate decision and further review of label limitations. Other possible extensions include a web \gls{ui}, \gls{pdf} export, and additional model interfaces. As Dehdashtian et al. distinguish diagnostic evaluation from mitigation \cite{dehdashtian2024survey}, any mitigation-facing work would also be a separate extension rather than a capability claimed here.
